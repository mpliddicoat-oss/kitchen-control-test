What allergen management software actually does

Strip away the marketing and it's a handful of jobs: hold an allergen profile against every ingredient, work out which allergens end up in a finished recipe or dish without you re-typing them each time, keep that up to date the moment an ingredient changes, and produce something you can hand to a customer or an inspector — a printed sheet, a matrix, or a PPDS label for anything sold prepacked.

Everything else — invoice scanning, costing, stock, staff training, QR menus — is a different job that some of these products bundle in alongside allergen tracking, and some don't. Worth knowing which is which before you compare on price.

Allergen management software: common questions

What is allergen management software?

Software that tracks which of the 14 regulated allergens are present in each ingredient and recipe, then rolls that up automatically into an allergen matrix and PPDS labels — instead of someone maintaining a spreadsheet by hand and updating it every time a recipe or supplier ingredient changes.

What are the 14 allergens I need to track under UK law?

Celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, nuts, peanuts, sesame, soybeans and sulphur dioxide/sulphites. UK food businesses must be able to tell a customer which of these are in any dish, whether it's on a menu, over a counter or on a PPDS label.

Does allergen software replace a spreadsheet allergen matrix?

It does the same job — a grid of dishes against the 14 allergens — but builds it automatically from ingredient data rather than someone re-checking and re-typing it every time a recipe changes. See our spreadsheet comparison for where a spreadsheet still holds up and where it starts to break down.

Can allergen data update automatically when a recipe or ingredient changes?

In Kitchen Control, yes — allergens are tracked at ingredient level, so changing a recipe's ingredients or scanning a new ingredient label updates every affected dish's allergen status and PPDS label immediately.

Does allergen software cover PPDS labelling too, or just an allergen matrix?

That depends on the product — some tools stop at the matrix. Kitchen Control generates full PPDS labels from the same recipe data, since a label is really the allergen matrix plus the ingredients list in the specific format Natasha's Law requires. See our PPDS & food labelling software comparison for that side of it specifically.

Is allergen software worth it for a single small kitchen?

If you're a genuinely small, one-person operation who already knows every dish's allergens from memory, a spreadsheet or even a written list can work. It starts paying for itself once a second person needs the same answer, ingredients change often enough that manual updates get missed, or an inspector wants to see something more robust than a bent-into-shape spreadsheet.

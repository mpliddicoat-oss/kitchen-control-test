What PPDS labelling software actually does

Natasha's Law requires food that's prepacked for direct sale (PPDS) — packaged before a customer orders it, on the premises it's sold from — to carry a full ingredients list with the 14 regulated allergens emphasised, not just a verbal allergen answer. PPDS labelling software takes the recipe you've already built for costing and allergen purposes and turns it into that label automatically, rather than a separate typing job every time.

For the full detail on what counts as PPDS and what has to be on the label, see our Natasha's Law guide. This page is about the software side: what the real options actually print, and how they compare.

PPDS & food labelling: common questions

What is PPDS labelling software?

Software that generates the full ingredients label required under Natasha's Law for food that's prepacked for direct sale — a complete ingredients list in descending weight order with the 14 regulated allergens emphasised, built from the recipe already in the system rather than typed out by hand each time.

What counts as PPDS food?

Food that's packaged before a customer orders it, on the same premises it's sold from, ready for the customer to buy without opening the packaging first — a sandwich made that morning and put in a chiller cabinet, for example. Food made to order across a counter isn't PPDS; it's covered by ordinary allergen-information rules instead. Full detail in our Natasha's Law guide.

Do I need a special printer for PPDS labels?

No specific brand is legally required, but a standard label printer such as a Brother QL is the common choice for kitchens printing labels themselves, since it prints directly from a browser without special software. See our label printing guide for setup detail.

Can label software update automatically when a recipe changes?

In a properly built system, yes — the label is generated directly from the recipe's current ingredient list, so if an ingredient or allergen changes, the next label printed reflects it automatically rather than relying on someone remembering to update a stored template.

Natasha's Law & PPDS Labelling, Explained

What the law actually requires, what counts as PPDS food, and how Kitchen Control's PPDS Label feature builds a compliant ingredients list — with allergens emphasised — straight from a recipe you've already built.

Kitchen Control · Guides

What is Natasha's Law?

Natasha's Law is the everyday name for the Food Information (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2019, in force since 1 October 2021. It's named after Natasha Ednan-Laperouse, who died in 2016 after an allergic reaction to a baguette bought from a shop that made and packaged it on the premises — food that, at the time, didn't legally need a full ingredients list because of how and where it was packed.

The law closed that gap. Food that's prepacked for direct sale — PPDS food — now has to carry the same kind of ingredients list as anything sold pre-wrapped from a supermarket shelf.

What counts as PPDS food?

PPDS (Prepacked For Direct Sale) food is packaged on the same premises it's sold from, before a customer orders or selects it. In a typical kitchen, that covers things like:

  • Sandwiches, wraps or rolls made up in advance and put in a chiller or display case
  • Cakes, traybakes or pastries wrapped and put out on a counter
  • Ready meals or salads packaged and shelved for grab-and-go
  • Pizza or other food sealed cold for takeaway before it's ordered

It generally does not cover food wrapped in front of the customer after they've ordered it, food made to order and served immediately, or loose deli-counter items weighed at the point of sale — though the exact line can depend on your setup, so it's worth checking against official FSA guidance if you're unsure.

What has to be on a PPDS label

The specific requirement Natasha's Law added is:

  • The name of the food
  • A full ingredients list, in descending order of weight
  • The 14 legally recognised allergens emphasised within that list — by bold, capitals, italics, underline or colour contrast, wherever they appear
Also worth knowing A business name and address isn't a legal requirement on a PPDS label — but it's widely considered best practice, since it makes the label traceable back to whoever made the food. Worth including even though it's optional.

What you're still responsible for

Kitchen Control's PPDS Label reflects whatever's recorded against your ingredients and recipes — it's only as accurate as that data. A few things worth checking before you rely on it:

Read before you print
  • A compound ingredient's own Full Ingredients List (e.g. a bought-in sauce's pasted-in ingredients) isn't scanned word-by-word for allergens — only allergens you've actually ticked against that ingredient get picked up and emphasised.
  • Your business's name and address only appear on the label once you've entered them in Settings — until then, that line is simply left off rather than printed blank.
  • It's not a substitute for your own HACCP plan, staff training, or a qualified food safety consultant if your operation needs one.

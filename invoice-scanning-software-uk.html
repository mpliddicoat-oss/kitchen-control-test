What invoice scanning software actually does

The basic job is simple to describe and easy to do badly: read a supplier invoice — photographed, emailed or uploaded — and extract the line items, quantities and prices without someone typing them in. The harder, more useful job is what happens next: does that scanned price actually flow through to update the ingredient's cost, and from there every recipe that uses it? A lot of invoice-scanning tools stop at "extracted the data" and leave the costing update as a separate manual step.

What to actually check before you buy

Invoice scanning: common questions

What does invoice scanning software actually do?

It reads a photographed or uploaded supplier invoice, extracts the line items and prices, and updates ingredient records automatically — instead of someone typing each line into a spreadsheet or system by hand.

Does invoice scanning update recipe costs automatically?

In Kitchen Control, yes — once an invoice is scanned and ingredient prices update, every recipe using those ingredients recosts immediately, without a separate step. See our recipe costing comparison for how that fits together.

Can I scan a photo of a paper invoice, or does it need a PDF?

Kitchen Control reads a photograph taken on a phone as well as a digital file — most kitchens scan the paper invoice as it arrives with the delivery.
